## About

- **Our Story** *(page heading — h1)*
  - Eyebrow: Our Story
  - Title: Is There Dinner?
  - Text:
    Unity Provisions began with a simple but painful question: "Is there dinner?"

    Growing up, our founder Ryan knew what it was like when food was uncertain. Later, while volunteering at a local food pantry, he saw how hunger could hide behind quiet sacrifices: a parent choosing between groceries and other necessities, or a neighbor masking their need with a smile.

    When that pantry abruptly closed in 2024, Ryan saw just how fragile food assistance programs could be. He founded Unity Provisions to build something that could last.

    What began as a single branch at Boston Latin School has grown into a youth-led network of 35+ branches across 8+ countries. Student leaders have collected thousands of pounds of food and clothing, raised thousands of dollars, and built partnerships with organizations such as the Wang YMCA to keep community-based donation centers running.

    Today, Unity Provisions works to make sure no child, family, or community has to wonder again, "Is there dinner?"

## Projects

- **Our Projects** *(page heading — h1)*
  - Title: Our Projects
  - Subtext: We're developing tools that make it easier to find food resources and grow more with less.

- **Relief Route**
  - Title: Relief Route
  - Text:
    Relief Route is an interactive map designed to make food resources easier to find. It brings together donation centers, food banks, and other community resources that can be difficult to discover, along with information such as hours and current donation needs.

    Relief Route is still in development. Our goal is to make it easier for people to find food assistance and other support in their communities.
  - Embed: live interactive map (reliefroute.unityprovisions.org)
  - Button: Help Build Relief Route → opens an email

- **AgriScan**
  - Title: AgriScan
  - Text:
    AgriScan is a low-cost, easy-to-use crop optimization tool we're developing for small farms, home gardens, and communities with limited resources. It combines a database of crop needs (including water, sunlight, and soil pH) with field data to provide practical suggestions for improving plant health and reducing waste.

    Each unit can cover up to one acre and can be connected with others for larger growing areas. It is designed to be simple to set up and use in places such as backyards, school gardens, and community plots.

    Our goal is to make practical farming tools more accessible, reduce water use, and help address hunger at the root.
  - Image: product screenshot
  - Button: Help Build AgriScan → opens an email
